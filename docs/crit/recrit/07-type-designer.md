## 2. The five-second test

**870 px, day.** A pale chart with a very large, very good serif name. Within the first second the eye lands on "Ben Russell", in the second on the italic line beneath it, in the third on the blue tinted islands with upright depth figures, in the fourth on the cartouche: "CRAWL AND DATA INFRASTRUCTURE · PYTHON AND RUST". What the page says: a person who makes maps of the web and takes care of how they look; a developer, not a designer, because the figures are counts and the names are repositories. It does not say "Scrapy maintainer"; Scrapy Harbor is one small ring. It does say "surveys", and the hatched margin says "goes where the map runs out".

**360 px.** The phone edition is a redesign, not a reduction: the name fills the width, the thesis breaks into two lines at a size I can read at arm's length, there are eleven islands instead of twenty-two, "SCRAPY HARBOR" and "GAME ENGINE I." are set in condensed caps at ~10 px rendered, and the four cartouche lines are legible (~9.5 px). It says the same thing as the desktop in the same order; the only thing lost is the rose's variation note, which is fine. "Profile Shoal" is the second largest piece of italic on the phone and its "fi" is a collision (see defect 4).

**Night.** The name recedes to a pale grey and the four lights come forward; the page reads "chart at night" before it reads "Ben Russell", which is the right order for a chart and the wrong order for a résumé by about half a second. The thesis italic is a tier greyer than the name and still reads. Everything below 10 px rendered is a tier harder to read than by day because the ink is lower-contrast and the stems bloom at the same time.

## 3. Top 5 defects, ranked

### 1. The Instruments sheet sets all its semantic text under the floor
- **Where.** instruments-day.png, columns at x 330–870, y 30–160 (crop instruments-columns.png). The mono list ("Python ……… Sep 2025") renders at ~8.1 px (cap-height 5.7 px at 870; native ≈ 12 px), the dates in condensed at ~7.5 px, the column heads "LEAD · LANGUAGES" at ~7 px, the footnote "what measures depth / bold · carried by a repository underway this quarter" at ~7 px. The three rotated words take 300 px of the 1280 for three words. At night (instruments-night.png) the grey ink takes the list below legibility. The phone edition (instruments-phone.png, 720×48) is a rule and "CHART NO. 21 · SHEET 5" with nothing above it: an empty frame.
- **Why it matters.** STANDARDS 5 (semantic text ≥ 13 px at 1280, ≈ 9 px rendered) and 8 (no empty still frame). This sheet carries the languages and stack: the information J10 is about, in the one place on the page where it is set too small to read. The HTML line under it rescues the recruiter, which means the sheet is decoration.
- **Fix.** Narrow the LEAD / LOG / LOOKOUT column to 200 px (the words are 46 px caps; they do not need 100 px each of air). Set the list in Plex Mono at 14 native (9.5 rendered), the dates in Plex Sans Condensed at 14, the column heads at 13 with tracking 0.06 em, the footnote at 13. Replace the leader dots with a 0.5 px rule at baseline −0.25 em (dots at 8 px are a grey smear; a rule survives). On the phone edition, either show the three rotated words with the three lists stacked beneath at 18 native, or do not emit a phone sheet for Instruments at all and let the HTML line stand; a rule with a sheet number is the worst of the three.
- **Cost.** M.

### 2. Fine print on hero and footer is demoted twice: small *and* grey
- **Where.** Hero cartouche, hero-day.png (60–310, 400–450): the first two lines ("THE OPEN WEB…", "CRAWL AND DATA INFRASTRUCTURE…") are ~9.5 px rendered in full ink and read; the three lines beneath ("SOUNDINGS IN COMMITS · DATUM: MAIN", "CHART NO. 21 · EDITION 0.1.3 · NOV 2025 · IALA REGION B", "CORRECTED THROUGH NOTICE 9") are ~8 px *and* in lighter ink. Footer, footer-day.png: "Obstn rep. 2026 (PA)" ~8 px grey italic, "14 · good holding" ~8.5 px, "LIMIT OF SURVEY 2026" rotated ~7.5 px, "UNSURVEYED" ~7 px, "9 notices · corrected through Notice 9" ~8.5 px grey. At night each of these loses another step.
- **Why it matters.** STANDARDS 5 and J4: the datum, region and correction state are the lines a hydrographer reads to name the sheet, and they are the least legible text on the hero. Hierarchy needs one cue, not two; size already says "secondary", ink saying it again pushes the lines under the medium.
- **Fix.** In the hero cartouche, set the three secondary lines at 13.5 native (9.2 rendered) in the same ink as the first two; keep the hierarchy by size and by the existing blank line. Footer: "Obstn rep. 2026 (PA)", "14 · good holding" and the notices line at 13 native; "UNSURVEYED" and "LIMIT OF SURVEY 2026" at 14 condensed caps with 0.08 em tracking. Do not touch the italic soundings 9 8 7 5 4 3 (texture; they are correctly tiny).
- **Cost.** S.

### 3. Night is recoloured, not reweighted
- **Where.** hero-night.png at 870: "FashionDB Bank" (400–500, 300–310) and "Data Science Bank" (640–740, 435–445) in Instrument Serif Italic ~10 px rendered; stems bloom to ~1.5 px and the 25-unit hairlines vanish, so the words read as dashes. log-night.png: Plex Mono dotted zero at ~9 px fills its counter ("timeout 0%" reads "θ%"). approaches-night.png legend at ~9 px: grey on navy, stems blooming, the lightest 36 lines on the page. The name itself is fine (pale, large, hairlines intact at 120 px rendered).
- **Why it matters.** J11 asks for a night that is designed; STANDARDS 5 asks type to survive the medium. Light-on-dark text needs less weight than dark-on-light at the same size; serving the same outlines guarantees the small serif blooms and the mono's dot closes. This is the half of the night edition that distinguishes "designed" from "palette-swapped".
- **Fix.** svgkit emits outlines, so weight is adjustable per edition without new fonts: on the night build, for Instrument Serif runs ≤ 16 px native, add `stroke="<paper>" stroke-width="0.18" paint-order="stroke"` (a choke); for runs ≥ 96 px, none. For Plex Mono in the night log and instruments, vendor IBM Plex Mono Light (OFL) and map it in the night build only; keep Regular by day. Night small-ink at #C3CDDD rather than the current grey-blue, with the name at its present pale value so the lights stay brightest.
- **Cost.** M.

### 4. Two fitting faults on the most-looked-at italic
- **Where.** (a) hero-title.png, x 145–205: "I survey" — the italic capital I overhangs right by about a third of a word space and svgkit does not compensate, so at 870 the thesis opens "Isurvey a web…". It is the first line anyone reads after the name. (b) hero-phone.png (180–380, 760–810) and hero-day.png (190–260, 282–292): "Profile Shoal" sets "f" and "i" as separate glyphs; the f's hook lands on the i's dot. The Italic subset has the fi ligature and nothing fires it.
- **Why it matters.** STANDARDS 5 (type is a carrier of meaning here) and J1: the thesis line must read instantly, and "Isurvey" costs a re-read. A collision in the second-largest italic word on the phone edition is the kind of thing a type-literate reader sees first and forgives last.
- **Fix.** In svgkit's manual kern table for Instrument Serif Italic: pair (`I`, space) +110 units; while there, (`r`, `·`) +40 as before. Enable `fi` and `fl` substitution in the glyph mapper (one lookup over the run before advancing); re-check "Profile", "fix/144" is in the sans and unaffected. Then re-render the hero-title crop and confirm the word space after I matches the one after "survey".
- **Cost.** S.

### 5. The Approaches legend and notes are a quarter of the sheet at 9 px
- **Where.** approaches-day.png, y 480–640: two NOTES boxes (11 lines, ~9 px rendered) and a 36-entry legend in three columns at ~9 px (crop approaches-legend.png shows them at 2x). The title "Approaches to Scrapy Harbor" renders at ~19 px, one size below the hero's thesis, over a field of 180 italic soundings at ~8.5 px.
- **Why it matters.** STANDARDS 5 says 9–11 px is for texture only; here 47 lines of semantic text sit exactly at the floor, which means every anti-aliasing and night penalty pushes them under it. It is also the block that makes the sheet read "textbook diagram" rather than "chart": charts put the symbol key on a separate sheet (Chart 1) precisely so the sheet itself can breathe.
- **Fix.** Cut the legend to symbols that appear on the two charts (drop "Halo · by night" from the day legend and keep it on night; fold "Flare · marks a light" into "Light · its character"); that is ~28 entries, which fit in three columns at 14 native (9.5 rendered) in the same box height. Set the NOTES bodies at 13.5 native, keep the italic figures ("256–1024", "250 ms") as they are. Take the sheet title to 36 native (24 rendered) and let "Surveyed by rustmapper 2026 · Datum: main" stay at its current size: the title currently has less presence than the two box titles beneath it.
- **Cost.** M.

Also seen, below the fold of this list: the README table under the hero renders an empty header row (readme-light-00.png, y 718–732) because GitHub tables require one; make "Ben Russell builds" the header row (S). The four Fl 4s lights (J4) want three characters: Fl R 2.5s, Fl R 4s, Fl G 2.5s, Fl G 4s (S). The nine-item "five principles" list (J9): keep five, move the four PyPI lines into one line under the log sheet (S).

## 4. Three things that must not be touched

1. **Instrument Serif at 176 px with the italic thesis set directly beneath it.** hero-title.png: hairlines at 4.4 px, −3 tracking, the "R" and "u" kerned, the whole name sitting on one baseline with the serifs over the contour field. The thesis at 34, same face, italic, one tier greyer at night. This pairing is the page's signature and the only image of its kind on GitHub; every other decision on the page can move before this one does.

2. **Lettering by feature class, and the one sentence that declares it.** "upright figures are measured · sloping figures are not · underlined: above datum" in the legend, and then the sheets keep the promise: floating marks italic (R "2" SCOUT Fl R 4s), water names italic (Delta Lake, 429 Shoal, Data Science Bank, FashionDB Bank), doubtful things italic (Rep, ED, SD, PA, Wk '25), fixed and dry things upright (Grafana Lt · Fl 15s, SCRAPY HARBOR, GAME ENGINE I., the title blocks), commit soundings upright and URL soundings italic. A reader who has never seen a chart learns the rule from the page and is never lied to.

3. **IBM Plex Sans Condensed for soundings, cartouches and the Soundings sheet, with the mono kept for machine output.** The row "61 16 16 85 25 2 0 0 0 7 4 3 240 296 0 …" and the repository grid hold at 8.5–9.5 px rendered with a plain zero and tabular figures where the mono's dotted zero would have closed; the cartouche caps reach 9.5 px without wrapping. The mono now means one thing on the page: the log, the pip lines, the equipment list. The phone editions of Soundings and Log (soundings-phone.png, log-phone.png) are the proof that the system scales: four log rows at 18-equivalent, the curve with HW and LW annotated, nothing shrunk.

## 5. Does it look like a team spent months on it?

The hero, the Soundings sheet, the log and the footer do, and the phone editions more than anything: a team that redraws a chart for 360 px rather than scaling it is a team that has looked at its own work on a phone. The lettering system is a real one and it is applied, which is rarer than choosing good faces. What still looks like a template is the Instruments sheet, which is a three-column list with leader dots that happens to have three rotated words beside it, set at a size that admits nobody expected it to be read; and the lower quarter of Approaches, where a 36-row symbol key and two NOTES panels do what a textbook figure does. What looks like a plan that leaked onto the page is the README's "five" principles with nine items and the blank table header. None of these is a concept problem; they are sizes, a kern pair, a ligature lookup, one stroke in the night build and ten minutes of copy. Fix them and the page says what the type already promises: that this person knows a 25-unit hairline and a dotted zero behave differently at 8 px than at 176, and wrote the rules so the detail survives the medium.
