## 2. The five-second test

**870 px, day.** A very large serif name over a cream chart; an italic sentence under it that I read in full because it is the only italic line of its size; islands with numbers; a title block in small caps. I know in 5 s that this is Ben Russell, that he does something with surveying and the web, and that the page was made by someone who draws. I do not yet know what he does for a living, but the table one scroll down says "crawl and data infrastructure" within the next 5 s. The small caps (title block, top edge, rose caption) are grey and I skip them; they feel like fine print, which is wrong, because the field and the datum are in them.

**360 px, day.** Better than desk for the first 5 s: name, thesis, compass, and a title block in 26 px caps that says CRAWL AND DATA INFRASTRUCTURE · PYTHON AND RUST. The islands lose every name except GAME ENGINE I. and SCRAPY HARBOR, which is the right trade. The approaches sheet becomes a very tall image of a coastline with nothing to explain the symbols; the instruments sheet becomes a horizontal rule. On a phone the page's second half is three images that say less than the Markdown under them.

**Night.** The name arrives in pale ink and the lit marks arrive as pink points before anything else, which is the designed effect and it works. The thesis reads. The title block sinks to fine print and I cannot read the datum line without leaning in; the month letters on the soundings sheet vanish. Night is the better edition for the chart and the worse one for the captions.

## 3. Top 5 defects, ranked

### 1. The footer sheet and the Colophon have been swallowed into one unclosed `<details>` (S)
- **Where.** `README.md` line 199: the Colophon's *Editions* bullet ends mid-sentence at "Your system picks the edition through `<!-- picture:footer:start -->"; the footer `<picture>` follows inside the bullet; `<!-- picture:footer:end -->` appears twice; two `<details>` open, one closes. `readme-light.png` and `readme-dark.png` end at y≈5080 (original px) on the collapsed "▸ Colophon" summary; no footer image is visible on either render.
- **Why it matters.** STANDARDS thesis: "the footer is where the water finally goes over the edge" — nobody sees it unless they open a disclosure labelled "how the sheets are drawn". Standard 13: the VAR 14h layer resolves in the Colophon, and the Colophon's motion/variation/license bullets no longer exist. WCAG 1.3.1 / 4.1.2: a disclosure widget whose contents are half a sentence, an image and no end; AT users hear "Colophon, collapsed" and the page ends.
- **Cause.** `scripts/render_readme.py` line 262, `_PICTURE_RE = re.compile(r"<picture>.*?</picture>", re.S)`. The Colophon's inline code `` `<picture>` `` matched as the start of a picture block, the non-greedy match ran to the real footer's `</picture>`, and `migrate_pictures` wrapped and replaced the whole span.
- **Fix.** Anchor the regex to whole lines: `re.compile(r"^<picture>\s*$.*?^</picture>\s*$", re.S | re.M)`, and skip any match whose preceding character is a backtick. In `chart.toml` write the Colophon sentence as "through the picture element's media queries" (no angle brackets in inline code anywhere the renderer scans). Restore the Type / Editions / Motion / Variation / License bullets, close the `</details>`, keep the footer `<picture>` after it at top level. Add to `check.py` (strings tier): `count("<details>") == count("</details>")`, every `picture:<sheet>:start` appears exactly once, and none falls between a `<details>` and its `</details>`. Re-render `readme-*.png` and confirm the footer sits below the Colophon.

### 2. Day small text is below AA, and the gate cannot see it (S)
- **Where.** Token `muted` #6B7A90 on paper #F4EEE1 = 3.77:1, used by 11–13 px roles (`label`, `label-caps`, `texture`). Rendered: `soundings-day.png` month letters (30–350, 150–163) 2.76:1; "typical week 0" (520–620, 110–122) 1.60:1; `instruments-day.png` dates (455–500, 40–52) 2.79:1; `log-day.png` TIME header 2.77:1 and the "light" wind column 2.59:1; `hero-day.png` title block small caps (60–300, 405–445) 3.77:1. Night is not clear either: soundings month letters 2.57:1, hero title block 3.46:1 rendered. In the README column these glyphs are 7–9 px.
- **Why it matters.** WCAG 1.4.3 asks 4.5:1 for text under 18 px. These are not texture: the datum, the unit, the months, the dates each tool was first used, and the column headers of the log are the facts of the sheet. `scripts/checks/contrast.py` line 79 floors `muted` at 3.0 and its docstring says muted is for "captions, ≥24 px or texture only"; the roles file disagrees, so the gate reports 0 findings on a palette that fails.
- **Fix.** Day `muted` → #56657B (5.13:1 paper, 4.35:1 land) or #4F5E74 (5.70 / 4.84). Night `muted` → #98A7BF (7.16:1) so anti-aliasing at 8 px still lands above 4.5. Where a role at <24 px uses `accent` or `ok` as a fill (buoy labels, "ok" ticks), use #B8341E (5.12:1) and #1F6E42 (5.39:1) by day and keep the brighter fills for marks ≥24 px. In `contrast.py`, derive the floor from the roles that use the colour: 4.5 for any colour a `_DESK` role under 24 px uses, 3.0 otherwise. Re-measure on the PNGs, not the tokens: add the rendered-core sampling (40 most-different pixels, median) as a `frames`-tier check at 870 px for the five regions listed above.

### 3. "Five principles" is a list of nine (S)
- **Where.** `README.md` Notices block, items 6–9: four "rustmapper 0.1.x on PyPI" lines, all "8 Nov 2025"; `readme-light.png` y≈3870–3960. The heading gloss says *five principles*; `footer-day.png` bottom right says "9 notices · corrected through Notice 9"; the hero title block says "CORRECTED THROUGH NOTICE 9".
- **Why it matters.** Standard 9 (copy says what it means), Standard 10 (the five principles are the hiring manager's "how he works" list and they are now diluted by four changelog lines), and plain cognitive load: a skimmer counting on the gloss stops at five and misses nothing, but a screen-reader user hears "list, nine items" and four of them are the same sentence with a different patch number.
- **Fix.** In `render_readme.py`'s notices block, number only `kind = principle` entries; render releases as one `<sub>` line beneath the list: "Also entered: rustmapper 0.1.0–0.1.3 on PyPI, 8 Nov 2025." Keep the sheets' "Notice 9" if the notice register genuinely has nine entries, but then change the footer caption to "5 principles · 4 releases · corrected through Notice 9" so the three places agree.

### 4. The phone editions of Approaches and Instruments do not say what their alt and captions say (M)
- **Where.** `approaches-phone.png` (1080×1860): chart only; no legend, no rustmapper/Scrapy Harbor notes panels; the README caption under it reads "The legend on this sheet defines every symbol used on the page". `instruments-phone.png` (1080×72): a hairline and "CHART NO. 21 · SHEET 5"; its alt reads "Equipment list in three columns … the date each was first fitted."
- **Why it matters.** WCAG 1.1.1: alt must describe the image that is served; `<picture>` serves one alt for all sources, so at phone width the alt describes columns that do not exist. J5: on the device where a stranger most needs "point at any mark, one sentence says its job", the sentence is gone. Standard 8: no empty frame, and the phone instruments sheet is one.
- **Fix.** Approaches: put the legend into Markdown as a two-column `<details><summary>Symbols on this sheet</summary>` table under the image on every width (it is the accessibility statement of the page; it should be real text anyway), and move the "upright figures are measured · sloping figures are not" line into the visible `<sub>` caption. Instruments: give the phone sheet real content at 720×~560 — the three rotated words as column heads (LEAD / LOG / LOOKOUT, 30 px) with the six items each at `label` 26 px and dates at `texture` 18 px — so that the one alt is true for both editions. If the budget does not allow it, drop the phone `<source>`s for Instruments so the desk sheet (870 px → 83 px tall at 360) is served; a small true image beats a large empty one.

### 5. Buoy labels are texture-sized and vanish first (S)
- **Where.** `crops/hero-entrance.png`: `R "2" Fl R 4s` and `G "1" Fl G 4s` are `texture-italic` 11 px (≈7.5 px in the README column); red nun against cream renders 2.52:1 (`hero-day.png` 560–585, 335–350). `hero-phone.png`: the buoys are kept, their labels are dropped, so at 360 px the colour-and-shape pair has no number.
- **Why it matters.** IALA was designed so that number parity and shape carry the meaning without colour; the number is the CVD fallback, and it is the first thing to go. WCAG 1.4.11 (3:1 for meaningful graphics) and the chart's own legend promise ("G can · port, returning / R nun · starboard").
- **Fix.** Promote buoy labels to `label-italic` 13 px at desk and keep `R2` / `G1` (the number only) at 26 px on the phone hero, placed inside or immediately above the body. Give the nun and the can a 1 px `ink2` outline (they already have it on the approaches sheet; the hero's are flat fills). No palette change needed.

## 4. Three things that must not be touched

1. **The `<picture>` wiring.** Eight sources on the hero and six on every other sheet, ordered phone+reduce+dark → phone+reduce → phone+dark → phone → reduce+dark → reduce → dark → img, with the phone sheets of soundings/approaches/log/instruments/footer static by construction (0 `<animate>`) and a dedicated `hero-phone-still`. This is the pause control WCAG 2.2.2 asks for, built from the only lever GitHub leaves. Do not simplify it.
2. **Alt texts and their mirrors.** Six alts of 19–25 words, plain register, first word never "The", each ending on one fact; and the Markdown that makes them sufficient: "Ben Russell builds …" as the first cell of the first table, the stack as one `<sub>` line, the figures as marked `<!-- n:… -->` spans identical to the sheets. A screen reader now gets the whole profile with the images turned off.
3. **The legend as the page's convention statement and the night lights.** "upright figures are measured · sloping figures are not · underlined: above datum" in `crops/approaches-legend.png`, numbered and shaped IALA marks, and at night the magenta halos as the brightest objects on the page. Colour is already not the only signal for anything that matters; defects 2 and 5 are about letting the fallbacks survive small sizes, not about redesigning the encoding.

## 5. Does it look like months of work?

Yes, at desk, by day, for a sighted reader: the chart grammar is right, the type has four real sizes with meaning in the italic, the sheets have different bones, the motion is a chart surveying itself and every still is finished. The dual-register headings, the fact table and the plain alts are the work of someone who sat down and thought about the reader who cannot see the picture. What still looks like a template is the fine print — the same grey small caps in every corner of every sheet at a contrast that treats the datum, the months and the dates as decoration — and what looks like a diagram is the phone edition of the second half of the page, where Approaches becomes an unlabelled coastline and Instruments becomes a rule. And one bug undoes the ending: the footer, the sheet the thesis was built toward, is currently inside a collapsed Colophon, so the page on GitHub finishes on a disclosure arrow instead of water going over the edge. Fix the regex, lift the greys, make the lists honest, and this reads as a team's work in every edition.
