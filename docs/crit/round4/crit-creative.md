# Creative direction — round 4, premises not polish

Read: readme-light and readme-dark in 1000 px slices, readme-390 in 1500 px slices, all six sheets at 870 px day (hero, approaches also night, hero-phone), README.md, DESIGN.md, stats.json, log.json, and the owner's-advocate review from round 3 (so the twelve polish items there are not repeated).

## 1. Costume or conceit

**The conceit is inverted, and that is why three rounds of polish have not fixed the feeling.** The thesis is "I survey a web that is wrong about itself." A survey chart earns its place when the thing charted is the thing surveyed. What is charted here is his commit history. The hero is a bubble chart of 21 repositories (area = commits) wearing a chart's clothes; Sheet 2 is the GitHub contribution graph as a tide; Sheet 5 is the Stack row of the table rotated 90 degrees. None of the measured data on the page is about the web. The only sheet that is about the web, Sheet 3, is marked by its own legend as "Sloping · not measured" for every URL figure, and the hero's "sitemap.xml lies again — see Sheet 3" sends the stranger to a sheet of decorative numbers. The log's 12,440 URLs is "computed from settings". So: a page whose point of view is "I measure the web" contains zero measured facts about the web and 1,665 measured facts about commits. The costume is on the wrong body. That is a methodology problem, not a design problem, and the owner is right to name it as both.

**Five seconds.** A stranger gets: cream chart, enormous "Ben Russell", a good italic line, blue blobs with numbers, a compass. That is a point of view, and it is unlike anything on GitHub; the type (Instrument Serif against Plex Condensed) is genuinely good. Then the picture contradicts the line. The largest island is GAME ENGINE I. (512, an eight-day repo); second is Data Science Bank (a browser game); the harbor named in the thesis is a dashed inset lower-left; the survey vessel is a 10 px boat and an italic sea-name over an irregular lagoon with 87. Thirteen of the 21 islands were created on one afternoon (sweep of 2025-11-09 in stats.json) and the chart promotes them into places with names. The data is honest; the form inflates it.

**Where it is a template.** Everything below the hero is the standard profile README: a centered nav row of links, "X builds …", Languages, Stack, two project sections with bullets and a `pip install` block, "other projects", a collapsible "more", a colophon. The sheets are illustrations pinned into that template at the usual places. A world-class page would not have a template underneath; it would be the chart, and the prose would be its notes.

**Where it is pastiche.** The 30-row SYMBOLS legend on Sheet 3 (longer than the chart above it); "IALA Region B"; "SMALL CORRECTIONS 2026 — 5–173"; "Obstn rep. 2026 (PA)"; "corrected through Notice 9"; "SOUNDINGS IN COMMITS" repeated as a running head on every sheet; six identical neatlines; a footer sheet whose only content is a page-index box and one line. Each of these is a correctly rendered Admiralty mannerism with nothing of his behind it.

**Where it is an in-joke.** "Datum: main", "VAR 14h (2026)" (variation = busiest commit hour), the rose as a 24 h clock, Profile Shoal charting itself, "Wk '25 fix/144-lean-docker-extras" as a dead branch, stage names as lights ("G "3" ANALYZE Fl(2) G 10s"), "Health Ldg Lts 290°". Each needs fluency in both git and hydrography, and the decoding table is in a collapsed Colophon. Two of these are delightful second looks. Seven of them are a wall.

## 2. The strongest version of this idea, and the strongest alternative

**Keep.** The thesis line. The paper/ink/night tokens and the three typefaces. The honesty convention (upright = measured, italic = computed, underline = above datum, ED/Rep/SD/PA for doubt): this is a real methodological invention and the most original thing on the page, and it is currently hidden in the colophon. The nightly redraw. The unsurveyed hatch and "limit of survey", which is a thought-through image of a crawler's limits. The typed log as a texture. "The chart ends here. The web doesn't."

**Cut.** Five of the six sheets. The legend. The marginalia above. The repository register table. The commit tide. The simulated run. The template under the hero.

**Push harder.** (a) Change the subject: chart a survey. Nightly, for one domain he is allowed to survey (his own; and read-only lookups are enough), take the three witnesses rustmapper already uses: sitemap.xml (what the site says about itself), the CT log (every host that ever held a certificate), the Common Crawl index (every URL someone once linked). Three measured counts that disagree. That disagreement *is* the thesis, and it is measurable every night in an Action without crawling anyone. Islands become hosts and URL groups; soundings become URL counts; "sitemap says yes; the lead says no" becomes an upright figure with a date. The commit data drops to a small register in the margin, where it belongs. (b) Title the chart as a chart: the place, not the surveyor. "Approaches to Scrapy Harbor" at 176 px; "Surveyed by B. Russell · rustmapper 0.1.3" in the publisher's line. (c) Promote the honesty convention onto the sheet in one line: "upright measured · italic computed · ED existence doubtful", and let a stranger watch a few figures be italic. (d) Make the motion the mechanism: the sheet draws itself in crawl order, breadth-first from the seed, a frontier filling, with the limit of survey the last thing to appear and the reason printed (robots.txt, budget). (e) Make "limit of survey" true: it is where the crawl stopped, dated.

**The strongest alternative, briefed to a designer.** *Call it "The Vessel, in section."* One image: an engineering drawing of rustmapper, the way a shipyard draws a hull in section, drawn nightly and labelled only with claims that stats.json can source to a commit sha (it already holds `claims` with source file and sha: 256–1,024 permits from governor.rs, shards = num_cpus from lib.rs, a 250 ms governor tick, the CRC32-framed WAL, rendezvous hashing by registrable domain, the three seed sources). The hierarchy: the drawing at full column width; one italic line of thesis above it; under it, two names and one install line (`pip install rustmapper`); nothing else above the fold. Every label is a leader line to a part, in the condensed face, with the sha in small caps at the end of the line, so the drawing is also its own bibliography. The motion is the machine: a seed enters at the left, splits into shards, waits on a permit, is written to the WAL before it reaches redb, and is retired to the sitemap at the right; one token every few seconds, 96 s loop, SMIL opacity and transform only. The night edition is a cyanotype: white lines on Prussian blue, and the tokens are the lights. Scrapy Harbor is the second drawing, smaller, in plan: scout, analysis, summarize, Delta Lake, with the breakers drawn as breakers. Commit history appears once, as a date stamp on the title block ("drawn 7 Oct 2026, revision 173"). This tells a stranger what he builds in five seconds (a machine, with its parts named), is one of a kind, and has no decoration a measured claim cannot stand behind. A cheaper cousin, if the drawing is too much: the README as the sitemap rustmapper would write of him, `<urlset>` with 21 `<url>` entries, real `<lastmod>` from last commit and `<priority>` from commit-days, one hand-written red annotation; deadpan, honest, thirty minutes of build, but not stunning.

## 3. The single change

**Chart the survey, not the commits.** Make the hero's data the nightly measured disagreement of three sources about one domain, and demote repositories to a register. It fixes the conceit, the honesty and the point of view in one move, and the existing build (contours by count, upright/italic figures, unsurveyed hatch, nightly workflow) already knows how to draw it. If that is refused as too large, the fallback single change is item 3 below: retitle the chart as a place and make the thesis the display line, because that is the only one-afternoon change that alters what a stranger sees in five seconds.

## 4. Ranked changes, radical to incremental

1. **Chart the web, not the commits.** Reframe, L. Hero drawn from three read-only nightly lookups for one owned domain (sitemap.xml count, CT-log hosts, Common Crawl index URLs); islands are hosts and URL groups; the register of repositories moves to the margin. Why: a survey chart of the surveyor is a stats widget; a survey chart of a survey is the thesis.
2. **Six sheets to one chart.** Cut, M. Keep the hero (reframed) and the typed log (only if real, item 4); fold the harbour inset and the few needed symbols into the hero; cut Soundings, Instruments, Footer as sheets. Why: a single page with one great image reads as a point of view; six sheets with the same neatline read as a product catalogue of the conceit. Keep the footer's one line as plain text.
3. **Title is the place and the thesis, not the name.** Reframe, S. "Approaches to Scrapy Harbor" (or the surveyed domain) at display size, thesis line beneath, "Surveyed by B. Russell" in the publisher's line, nav row removed. Why: a 176 px name says nothing; charts are titled by what they chart; the name is already the URL.
4. **The log is the log of drawing the chart.** Reframe, M. Replace the computed run with the nightly build's own real run: stats.json already records provenance (clones live, GraphQL cached, "soundings taken 1 of 150"), the count of repositories cloned, the time taken, what fell back to cache. The page already claims "the chart is one of the things it charts"; make the log prove it. Why: the typed log is the best texture on the page and its content is currently theatre ("Nothing on fire." is a scripted beat).
5. **Draw order is crawl order.** Build, M. The hero's draw-in (currently 4 s of fade, then a boat for 24 s) becomes a breadth-first fill from the seed: seed, then sitemap-claimed URLs, then what was found, then the limit of survey last with its reason printed. Why: the only motion worth having is the mechanism; the boat is a cartoon.
6. **The page is a product; say so above the fold.** Build, M. The colophon's last line ("fork, fill in chart.toml, run the workflow; the sheets redraw from your repositories") is the most interesting fact about the build and it is collapsed at the bottom. Give the generator a name, one install line, and a single sentence under the hero. Why: it tells a stranger he builds infrastructure, by demonstration, and it is the one thing no other profile can copy without running his code.
7. **Two second-looks, not nine.** Cut, S. Keep the rose as a commit clock and the unsurveyed hatch; remove IALA Region B, Datum: main, Small corrections, Chart No. 21, the A/B/C box, "corrected through Notice 9", the footer page index. Why: each decoded only via the colophon; each costs a stranger a little trust.
8. **Legend to what is on the sheet.** Cut, S. Six symbols that appear on the hero, set in one line under the neatline; the 30-row SYMBOLS panel goes. Why: a legend longer than its chart is the single clearest pastiche tell.
9. **Night is a different reading, not a palette.** Reframe, S. At night a chart is read by its lights: suppress contours and soundings to hairlines, keep names, the course and the lights at full strength. Why: the current night edition is the same sheet in navy; a night edition that shows fewer things is a designed one.
10. **Retire the tide.** Cut, S. The 52-week commit curve is honest and says "most weeks zero, the peak is a game"; it is the contribution graph in costume. If cadence is wanted, one 52-week hairline sparkline beside each flagship's name in prose. Why: honesty is in what is measured, but also in what is chosen to be measured; commits are the wrong instrument for a crawl engineer.
