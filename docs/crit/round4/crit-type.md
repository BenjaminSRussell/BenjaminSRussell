# Typographer + composition critique — round 4

Evidence: `render/readme-light.png`, `render/readme-dark.png` sliced to `crit4/page-*-{0..4}.png`; every sheet at 870 (day and night); the hero re-rendered from `v9/hero-still-day.svg` and `hero-still-night.svg` at 1740 and quartered (`crit4/hero-1740-*.png`); both phone sheets. Measurements below are from the 870 day renders with PIL. Type is outlined, so sizes are taken from `DESIGN.md` × 0.68.

## Verdict in one paragraph

The type system is 80 % right and can be kept; the composition of the hero and of the page should be rethought, not polished. The three faces are defensible, the roles are chart-correct, and the night grades contain one real error. What makes the owner say "I still don't like the design" is not kerning: it is that the name is a wordmark dropped on top of a chart instead of being the chart's title; that the hero's left half is a scatter of twenty islets that no hand would have drawn; and that the page alternates between two typographic systems (GitHub Inter markdown and the sheets) eleven times, with the markdown headings repeating what the sheets already say and two sheets repeating what the prose already says. The sheets are drawn by one hand. The page is not.

## 1. The type system

**Do the three faces make one voice?** Nearly. IBM Plex Sans Condensed for labels and IBM Plex Mono for the log are the right engineer's faces for a chart: NOAA and UKHO label in a condensed grotesque, and a log is typed. Instrument Serif is the one I would question. It is a display face with no text cut, and the page uses it at 25 (17 px shown) for place names and notes, where its hairlines go spindly; at 1740 the thesis is handsome, at 870 "Profile Shoal", "FashionDB Bank", "rustmapper" are thin and slightly grey. It also carries a strong 2023–25 landing-page association (it is the Linear/Vercel-era serif), and that association is exactly what the hero's layout reinforces: huge serif name top-left, italic tagline under it, illustration right. A type director would either keep Instrument Serif for the 176 and 41 sizes only and set the 25-size serif roles in a face that has a text cut and a real italic (Newsreader at its 16-opsz, or Source Serif 4 — both free, both outline cleanly), or move everything serif to Newsreader (Display for the name) and accept a slightly less fashionable, more engraved tone. I would do the second: one serif with optical sizes is cleaner than two.

**Is the display setting of the name excellent or merely big?** Merely big. At 870 the name is 89 px cap-to-baseline and spans 50 % of the sheet width; the setting itself (−3 tracking at 176, the "ll" pair, the R leg) is competent and I have no letter-level complaint. The failure is relational: the name has no baseline, cap line or margin in common with anything else on the sheet. Its cap top (y≈62) does not meet the compass rim; its baseline (y≈151) meets nothing; the title block that belongs to it ("THE OPEN WEB · FROM SURVEYS 2025–2026 …") sits 230 px below in the bottom-left corner, detached, and is four lines of identical 13-px caps with no hierarchy of its own. On a real chart the name of the chart *is* the title block: one large line, then scale, then datum and edition in two smaller sizes, in a clear area or cartouche. Here the name is one thing and the title block is another, and the thesis italic floats between them. The name's top margin to the border (~30 px shown) is also cramped for type that size; the sheet gives the name less air than it gives the compass.

**Registers.** Caps condensed for land (islands: "COURSE CRUSADER I."), italic serif for water features ("Profile Shoal", "Data Science Bank"), upright serif for land names — that is correct chart grammar and I would not change the assignment. The problem is that the reader does not know the grammar, and the same class of object (a repository) appears in three registers: "GAME ENGINE I." (caps), "Profile Shoal" (italic serif), "rustmapper" (italic serif, lowercase), "SCRAPY HARBOR" (caps, larger), "FashionDB Bank" (italic serif, mixed). To a reader it looks like random variety. Either every repository is one kind of feature, or the legend on the hero says in one line which kind means what. The approaches sheet has this problem less because its labels are mostly marks, not repositories.

**Do they read at 870?** Semantic 19 → 13 px: yes, tightly. Texture 16 → 11 px: no, not as reading matter. The soundings strip's two staggered rows of weekly figures ("61 16 25 / 16 85 2"), the month letters, the per-repo mini-sparklines, the legend's third column, the subscript months on every island (512₁ → the subscript is ~7 px) are all 11 px or smaller. The honesty conventions (upright = measured, italic = illustrative, subscript = months/hundreds) are carried entirely by distinctions that are invisible at page scale. That is honest data nobody can read. The fix is not to enlarge texture to 18 (12 px); it is to halve the quantity of texture and raise what remains.

**Tracking and case.** Caps labels at +0.8/19 (4 %) are tight for a condensed grotesque in caps; chart caps are usually spaced 8–12 %, and the wider spacing is a large part of what makes a chart look like a chart. Set `label-caps` at +1.6 to +2.0 and the title block at +2.0. The sea-name role at +2.0 on an italic serif is a wrong instinct (tracked italics fight their own slant) — leave italics at 0. Display −3 and thesis −0.2 are fine.

**The 19 / 16 / 25 split.** Two sizes of condensed (13 and 11 shown) and one of serif (17 shown) is too flat a scale for a sheet this dense: the 13/11 difference is below the threshold where a reader perceives a second level. Prefer 19 / 25 for condensed (13 / 17 shown) and 25 / 41 for serif (17 / 28), with 16 reserved for genuine texture (contour figures only). One fewer size, more distance between the sizes that remain.

**Night grades — one real error.** `ink2` night is `#8894A6` and `muted` night is `#98A7BF`: the "muted" caption colour is *brighter* than the secondary-type colour. At day the order is correct (`#34465F` secondary is darker than `#56657B` muted). At night the hierarchy inverts: captions out-rank secondary labels. Swap them, or set muted to about `#6F7D93`. Separately, light-on-dark type reads optically thinner, and the 0.8 hairline and the Instrument Serif hairlines both drop out at night at 870 (visible in `hero-night-1740-tl.png`: the thesis italic's thins nearly vanish). Night needs its own weight table (HAIR 1.0, PEN 1.5) and, if the serif stays, a slightly heavier cut at night is not available — another argument for a serif with optical sizes.

## 2. Composition

**The hero as a first screen.** Where the eye goes: name (top-left) → the thick blue ring of Game Engine I. (right-centre) → the clock dial (top-right) → the hatched band (right edge). Scrapy Harbor and rustmapper, the two things the links line and the prose say matter, are fourth and fifth. The chart's visual hierarchy contradicts the page's narrative hierarchy, and that is a consequence of the method (area ∝ commits) that composition must then compensate for; the dashed "SEE SHEET 3" box is the compensation and it is too weak to win. Ink density by quadrant: TL 0.17, TR 0.17, BL 0.16, BR 0.31. The sheet is bottom-right heavy; diagonal compositions can work, but here the diagonal has nothing on its upper-left end except the name, and the middle — between the thesis and Game Engine — is filled with islets ("9₂", "22₃", "8₂", "37₃", "12₂", "4₂", "17₂", "20₂" …) that form no coast, no archipelago, no current. That scatter is the single strongest tell of a generated drawing. Compare the phone hero (`hero-phone-360.png`), which has seven islands instead of twenty and reads as a composed sheet; and the approaches sheet, which has a coast and is far the best-composed sheet for that reason.

What the empty paper does: there is almost none. The hero is 20 % ink and the paper is evenly consumed; the name has no clearing. The hatched "UNSURVEYED" band is the heaviest texture on the sheet and sits on the outer edge, so the eye is pulled off the sheet rather than into it. The clock dial dressed as a compass rose (an "N" and a red triangle above a 24-hour dial with a hand) is the second-heaviest object and competes with the name from the opposite corner; it is a mixed metaphor at first glance and a nice joke at second, and a hero should not need a second glance for its second-largest element.

Title block vs name: see §1. They are one object on every real chart and two objects here.

**Each sheet.**
- *Soundings*: a sparkline over a 5-column table of 21 repositories with per-row mini-sparklines and underlines. It is a table pretending to be a chart; the mini-sparklines are noise at 870, the staggered figure rows are illegible, the month axis is 11 px. Its one good move, the HW/LW annotations, is lost under the clutter.
- *Approaches*: the best sheet. A coast, a lead line, a titled cartouche, a confidence table, a shoal with figures. Its weakness is that the symbols legend is 40 % of the sheet's height, in 13 px, three columns — a reference table riding on the one sheet that could have been a picture. The legend belongs in the colophon or a sheet of its own, and only the half-dozen symbols this sheet actually uses belong on it.
- *Ship's log*: coherent in itself, the typewriter on ruled paper is a good register, but the "different paper" (`#F8EEDB` vs `#F4EEE1`) is invisible — commit to a visibly warmer sheet or drop the token. On the page it sits 300 px under a GitHub code block (`pip install rustmapper …`) that shows the same commands in a second monospace: two typewriters, two faces.
- *Instruments*: three dotted-leader columns, Plex Mono caps headings. Its content — languages, stores, deck — is already in the prose line under the hero ("Languages Python, Rust; Swift … Stack Delta Lake, PostgreSQL …"). The sheet adds only first-commit months. It is the second-weakest sheet and the most redundant.
- *Footer*: one italic line, a boat, a sheet index, the hatch, air. At page scale it is the best-looking sheet, because it has one idea and room. That is the lesson for the hero.

**The page's rhythm.** hero (503) → links → prose → H2 → sheet (381) → H2 → sheet (606) → prose, bullets, code → H2 → sheet (401) → H2 → list → H2 → list → H2 → sheet (256) → details → sheet (200). Six cream frames of the same width, same dashed border, same plate, similar density, stacked: a run of postcards. Between them, GitHub's Inter H2 with an `<hr>` — and each H2 repeats the sheet's own caption directly beneath it: "Soundings" over "SOUNDINGS IN COMMITS · DATUM: MAIN"; "Approaches" over "Approaches to Scrapy Harbor"; "Ship's log" over "Log of the rustmapper"; "Instruments" over "DATUM: FIRST COMMIT". The sheets already carry their headings; the markdown headings are a second typographic voice saying the same words.

**One hand?** The six sheets, yes — border, plates, palette, hatch, the sloop, all one hand. The page, no: the prose is a stock README (bold-lead bullets, a code block, numbered list, blue links) and never adopts anything from the chart, and the chart never cedes anything to the prose. The dashed border itself is the most template-like element on the page: at 870 the alternating navy/white blocks read as a film strip or a CSS `dashed` border, not as the graduated minute border they imitate (hairline ticks with subdivisions and figures).

## 3. Colour and night

Cream `#F4EEE1`, navy `#1B2A41`, two pastel blues `#D8E7F1` / `#BEDAEC`, red/green laterals, magenta flare: this is the generic "vintage nautical" palette of every chart-themed mock-up, and the pastel blues on cream in particular read gift-shop rather than Admiralty. The actual signature of a chart is paper-white or buff, one black ink, one cool blue tint, and chart magenta for everything lit and important — and magenta is the one colour here used only for 4-px flares. Two honest options: (a) desaturate the water tints toward one cooler, slightly greyer blue (`#CFE0EB` / `#B4CFE0`) and lift the paper toward `#F7F3EA`; or (b) keep the cream and give magenta a real job (the lead line, the "SEE SHEET 3" inset, the limit of survey), so the palette has one voice that is not pastel. The land tone `#E9DFCA` is nearly paper at 870; islands rely on their outline, which is fine at day and fails at night.

Night is designed in intent and swapped in execution. The DESIGN.md claim — "the lights are the brightest things on the sheet" — is true, and the magenta flares at night are the one genuinely designed night element. Everything else is the day drawing with palette substitution and the day weight table: the hairlines vanish, the serif thins vanish, `land` `#1F2730` against paper `#0F1A2B` barely separates (islands lose their fill), the two water tints `#10294A` / `#123A5E` against `#0F1A2B` are almost indistinguishable at 870 (`soundings-night`, `hero-night`), so the chart loses the land/water distinction a chart exists to show, and the muted/ink2 inversion (§1) scrambles caption hierarchy. Night needs: its own weights (+20 %), a land tone two steps lighter than paper (`#263040`), water tints with real separation (`#13304F` / `#1A4470`), and the grade swap. It does not need a new idea; it needs the day idea redrawn at night, as the editions doc promises.

## 4. The single change

**Make the name the chart's title: fold it into the title block, give that block hierarchy, and let the chart be composed around the harbor, not around the type.** Concretely: one cartouche top-left — the name at 176 (or 141), the thesis italic as its second line, then the four existing title-block lines at two sizes (25 and 19, caps tracked +2) directly beneath, aligned to one left edge with one baseline grid; the right 60 % of the sheet becomes the chart, drawn with a coast and the harbor as its subject; the islet scatter is edited down to what the phone edition already shows. This single change fixes the "merely big" name (it becomes a title with a block under it), the detached title block, the void in the middle of the sheet, and the hero/narrative hierarchy conflict — the harbor becomes the picture, the name becomes the title of the picture. It is one morning's work in the generator because every element already exists; only the placement rules change.

## 5. Ranked changes

1. **Hero rebuilt as title-block + chart** (radical, L). Name, thesis and the four title lines become one aligned cartouche with three sizes; the chart occupies the remaining area with the harbor as subject; the mid-sheet islet scatter cut to the phone edition's set. Why: §4.
2. **Give the hero a coast** (radical, L; part of 1 or its own step). A continuous shoreline (the author's mainland of work, with the harbor as its port) organises the sheet, gives the name land to sit on as charts do, and makes the water mean something. Approaches already proves this: it is the only sheet with a coast and the only one that composes. Needs a methodological decision about what the coast represents — do not fake it.
3. **Cut the markdown H2s that repeat the sheets' own titles** (page, S). "Soundings", "Approaches", "Ship's log", "Instruments" go; the sheets' top captions are already the headings. Keep "Notices to mariners" and "Other waters", which head prose. Removes four of the eleven voice changes at zero cost.
4. **Cut the Instruments sheet; cut either the log sheet or the code block** (page, S). Instruments duplicates the Languages/Stack line; the log and the `pip install` block are two typewriters saying the same thing. Five sheets, no duplication, and the stack of same-width postcards becomes hero / strip / chart / log / footer with real variety.
5. **Night redrawn, not swapped** (M). HAIR 1.0 / PEN 1.5 at night; land `#263040`; tints `#13304F` / `#1A4470`; swap `ink2` and `muted` night values so secondary type out-ranks captions. Why: islands and shoals vanish at night and the caption hierarchy is inverted.
6. **Move the symbols legend off the Approaches sheet** (M). Keep on-sheet only the symbols that sheet uses (eight lines); the full table goes to the colophon or a seventh, narrower sheet. Approaches regains 40 % of its height for the picture.
7. **Halve the texture, raise the rest** (M). Drop subscript months/hundreds at desk scale (7 px is unreadable; the convention is dead weight), drop per-row mini-sparklines on Soundings, drop the staggered weekly-figure rows; what remains is set at 19 (13 shown) minimum. Honest data a reader cannot read is not honesty.
8. **One serif with optical sizes** (M). Newsreader (Display for 176/141, 16-opsz for 25) in place of Instrument Serif, or Instrument Serif kept for display only with Newsreader at 25. Why: Instrument Serif has no text cut and its hairlines fail at 17 px shown and at night; it also dates the page to the landing-page serif of 2023–25.
9. **Tracked caps** (S). `label-caps` +1.6, title block +2.0, sea-name italic back to 0. A tracked condensed cap is the chart look; a tracked italic is not.
10. **Replace the dashed block border with a graduated hairline border** (S–M). Double hairline with fine ticks and sparse figures, as a chart's minute border; the alternating navy blocks read as a CSS dashed border at 870 and are the most template-like mark on the page. If the ticks can carry data (months along the top of the soundings strip), better; if not, hairline ticks alone are already an improvement.

Not on the list because they are polish, not design: the clock-as-compass (make it smaller and move it below the title block once 1 is done); the invisible log paper tone (`#F8EEDB` → commit or drop); the links line under the hero duplicating the plate's URL.
