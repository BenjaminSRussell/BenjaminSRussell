# The Stranger's cold read — BenjaminSRussell profile page

Panel role: design-literate engineer, no brief, no source, no docs. Desktop render first (1100 px wide), then the 390 px phone render. Notes written at each stage before moving on.

## Step 1 — five seconds, first screen only (top 1000 px)

**What is this.** A GitHub profile README dressed as an Admiralty-style nautical chart. Cream paper, dashed chart border, compass rose, blue bathymetric tints, "CHART NO. 21 · SHEET 1" in the margin. It's immediately legible as "a chart" and not immediately legible as "a profile page" — I had to find the name in the big serif to confirm.

**Who.** Ben Russell. The tagline under the name — *"I survey a web that is wrong about itself."* — and the sub-line *"sitemap.xml lies again — see Sheet 3"* did more for me in two seconds than anything else on the sheet. That is a person who crawls websites and has been burned by them. Good.

**What they do.** Chart marginalia says "CRAWL AND DATA INFRASTRUCTURE · PYTHON AND RUST". The prose under the chart says it plainly: web crawlers, discovery pipelines, raw-first storage, dashboards. Languages and stack listed in a single dense paragraph. So: backend / data-infra engineer, crawling specialism.

**What I feel.** Delight first, honestly — it's a handsome object and someone clearly cared. Then a quick wobble of suspicion: the islands have numbers with little subscripts (512₁, 308₄, 173₄, 87₆) and names like "Game Engine I.", "Go Go Go I.", "Profile Shoal", "Data Science Bank". I assumed "I." = Island and the numbers are commit counts because the border says "SOUNDINGS IN COMMITS", but I was guessing, and the subscripts meant nothing to me yet. The cartography pulled focus from the content for the first couple of seconds. The "VAR 14h (2026)" compass and "IALA REGION B" are pure flavour; I skipped them.

**What I'd click.** `rustmapper` — it's the first link, it's bold, and the chart has a label "rustmapper" sitting over on the right near "Limit of survey 2026", so the page is nudging me there. Second choice: "Scrapy Harbor", because it has an anchor symbol, a dotted box and "SEE SHEET 3", i.e. the page itself says that's where the action is.

## Step 2 — thirty seconds, screens two and three (1000–3000 px)

**Soundings (Sheet 2).** A 52-week commit sparkline with a twist: high water / low water / slack water annotations ("HW 358 · wk of 5 Oct · Data Science Bank", "LW 0 · wk of 24 Aug", "typical week 0"). Below it a grid of 21 repos with commit counts, 512 down to 4. The weekly numbers along the bottom are tiny and I did not read them. I did read the footer, which is the most trustworthy line on the page: *"21 public repositories cloned, author's commits only · 1,665 commits · 1,966 all hands · 1,828 by GitHub's calendar."* That's someone showing their work and the discrepancies between three counts. I liked that more than the chart it sits under. What I now understood: most commits are in two or three repos, activity is spiky (a huge January spike, almost nothing March–July, busy again in autumn). What that tells me as a stranger: this is a side-project / student-ish cadence, not a 9-to-5 contributor graph. Not a criticism, just what the data says.

**Approaches to Scrapy Harbor (Sheet 3).** This is the page's big set piece: a harbour approach chart where the buoys are pipeline stages (R "2" SUMMARIZE, G "3" ANALYZE, G "1" URLS, R "4" SCOUT), the lighthouse is Grafana, the anchorage is Delta Lake, the dock-side labels are Redis · queues, PostgreSQL · metrics, Prometheus, stage1_discovery / stage2_page_analysis / stage4_summaries. There's a "429 Shoal" (rate-limit joke, got it, smiled), a "robots.txt · Disallow" restricted area, a "sitemap says yes; the lead says no" annotation, and "Zones of confidence A sitemaps · existence doubtful / B CT logs · exists, may not answer / C Common Crawl · as reported". The symbol key underneath translates every chart glyph into a system concept (G can = port, returning; Wk = dead branch, year; ED = existence doubtful; Obstn = reported obstruction; Hatch = unsurveyed).

What I understood: the architecture is a staged Scrapy pipeline with queues in Redis, raw storage in Delta Lake, metrics in Postgres/Prometheus/Grafana, seeded from sitemaps, CT logs and Common Crawl, and the author has a specific, slightly bitter theory that sitemaps lie. What I skipped: the depth numbers scattered across the water (18, 21, 24, 29, 33, 39, 41, 43...) — "SOUNDINGS IN URLS · THOUSANDS" says they mean something, but I couldn't make them mean anything, and I stopped trying. What looked like decoration: compass, variation, IALA region, chart number, "marks numbered from seaward", most of the symbols key. What looked like evidence: the zones-of-confidence box, the stage names, the service labels, the 429 shoal.

**rustmapper and Scrapy Harbor prose.** This is where I decided the person is real. Five bullets for rustmapper that read like someone who built it, not someone who prompted for it: throttle governed by redb commit latency sampled every 250 ms, worker pool 256–1,024 permits, CRC32-framed WAL with rkyv zero-copy replay, rendezvous-hashed frontier by registrable domain one shard per core, robots.txt crawl-delay honoured, seeds from sitemaps/CT logs/Common Crawl, maturin wheel for Apple silicon on CPython 3.13 and an honest note that everywhere else `pip` builds from source and needs a Rust toolchain. Then a four-line install/run snippet. Scrapy Harbor gets the same treatment: scout spider first, raw pages in Delta Lake with typed Arrow schemas and OPTIMIZE/VACUUM from a maintenance queue, dedup by URL hash and MinHash, BART-large-CNN summaries on the worker so nothing leaves for an API, circuit breakers around HTTP/Delta/Redis, Compose and Helm.

**Belief at thirty seconds.** A crawl-infra engineer, strong Python + Rust, happiest designing for failure (WAL, breakers, raw-first), with one published package (rustmapper, 0.1.3, "provisional") and one platform repo. The page says "provisional" and "edition" a lot, which reads as self-aware rather than grand. The map is the costume; the bullets are the body.

## Step 3 — two minutes, the whole page plus phone

**Ship's log (Sheet 4).** A fixed-width ship's log where the columns are TIME · LOG · URLS · SPEED · REQ/S · WIND · REMARKS, and the entries are an actual rustmapper session: pip install, crawl against 127.0.0.1:8080 with 512 workers, "seed list · sitemap · 1 host · robots.txt read · delay 0.5 s · frontier 8 shards · wal on", fetched 230, "remarks · nothing to report", "Nothing on fire.", crawl complete 12,440 urls, export sitemap. Closing line: *"Log closed 1550 · computed from settings · unsigned."* The "computed from settings" tells me this is a reconstructed/illustrative run, not a captured one. I respect that it says so, but it also slightly deflates it: for a moment I thought I was reading a real log. The "WIND" column is empty, which is a joke I appreciated more on the second look than the first. I found the ship's log the most charming single artefact on the page — the "Nothing on fire." line in particular.

**Notices to mariners.** Five engineering principles, each pinned to a repo and a month: boring under load (breakers), keep the log / raw before clean (Delta tables, WAL), lights before speed (dashboards in v1), parse don't pattern-match (ideal-url-organizer), and "the surface is part of the system — this page, Nov 2025: its earlier editions read as a template." That last one is the author telling me, obliquely, that this page was redesigned because the old one looked generic. I read it as self-aware; a less charitable reader would read it as a bit precious. Then "Notices 6–9, editions: rustmapper 0.1.0 · 0.1.1 · 0.1.2 · 0.1.3" — release links in costume. "Found a wrong depth? Open an issue." Nice.

**Other waters.** Four more repos with one-line pitches: ideal-url-organizer (25+ URL sorting strategies, "no regex" rule), go_go_go (Go sibling of rustmapper, Bloom filter sized for 100M+ URLs), rust_llm_logger (non-buffering reverse proxy that stream-tees tokens for metrics), Ai_code_detector (stylometry + git-history forensics for AI-generated code). A collapsed "Below the waterline · 15 more repositories: Swift widgets, a C game engine, games, tooling". Good triage: the author knows what's load-bearing and what isn't, and didn't make me scroll through the Swift widgets.

**Instruments (Sheet 5).** Three dotted-leader columns, "DATUM: FIRST COMMIT": each language/store/tool with the month it first appears in the commits. Python Sep 2025, Rust Oct 2025, Swift Jan 2026, Go Nov 2025, Delta Lake Sep 2025, redb Oct 2025, MLX · Qwen Nov 2025. This is the single most honest thing on the page and I almost scrolled past it because it's the least decorated. It tells me the whole public body of work is about 13 months old. That recontextualises everything above: the depth of the rustmapper bullets is impressive *for thirteen months*, and the page never tries to hide that; it just doesn't lead with it.

**Colophon** (collapsed) and **Sheet 6**: "The chart ends here. The web doesn't." with a little sailboat, "Obstn rep. 2026 (PA)", "LIMIT OF SURVEY 2026", "UNSURVEYED" hatching, "corrected through Notice 9". Sign-off. Cute, one screen too long, but it's the last thing on the page so it costs nothing.

**Phone (390 px).** Genuinely better than I expected. The title chart is re-laid for portrait and marked "SMALL-SCALE EDITION": fewer islands, bigger labels, the same four big numbers, the compass tucked under the name. The Soundings sparkline keeps its HW/LW annotations and drops the per-week numbers (correct call). The Approaches chart is *rotated* — "← N" with north to the left — and reflowed as a tall harbour with the symbol key stacked in two columns underneath; everything is legible without pinching. The ship's log is cut to four entries plus a summary line ("1947 · 1,665 commits · 21 repos"). The prose and bullets are unchanged. The only phone complaint: the Instruments sheet runs to a very tall column and the code block's trailing backslash line wraps awkwardly, but that is a GitHub thing, not the page's.

### Three things I now know for certain about this person's work

1. They build web crawlers and the infrastructure around them, in Python and Rust, and they think about them as systems that must survive being left alone: write-ahead logs, circuit breakers, raw-first storage, dashboards in version one.
2. They have shipped one real artefact to a public registry — rustmapper on PyPI, four releases, 0.1.x, with the install caveats stated out loud — and one larger platform repo (Scrapy Harbor) that the page describes in enough operational detail that I believe it runs.
3. The public body of work is young (first commit Sep 2025, 1.6k own commits over 21 repos, two or three of them carrying most of the weight) and the author's cadence is bursty, not steady.

### To a colleague, in one sentence

"Someone built their GitHub profile as an Admiralty chart where the buoys are pipeline stages and the depth soundings are commit counts — it's the most fun profile I've opened this year, and underneath the costume the rustmapper writeup is actually good."

### Confusing

- The subscripted numbers on Sheet 1 (512₁, 87₆, 46₄). The key on Sheet 3 eventually explains "265₅ Height · commits, months" — so the subscript is active months. Fair, but it's explained two screens after it's used, and I had already given up on it.
- "SOUNDINGS IN URLS · THOUSANDS" on the Approaches chart: the numbers in the water never resolve to anything I could check. They're texture pretending to be data.
- "Soundings in commits · Datum: main" on three different sheets. "Datum" is doing a lot of work for a nautical word. I got it on the third pass.
- The title chart's island names: "Course Crusader I.", "Go Go Go I.", "Data-Visualizer I." are repo names; "Profile Shoal", "Data Science Bank", "FashionDB Bank" are also repo names but with chart suffixes. Mixed scheme — some are repos-as-islands, some are repos-renamed-as-features — and I couldn't tell which was which without the Sheet 2 table.
- "VAR 14h (2026)" — I assume it's a pun on magnetic variation, probably a timezone offset or something. Never resolved. Doesn't matter.

### Fun

- The 429 Shoal. "sitemap says yes; the lead says no." "robots.txt · Disallow" drawn as a restricted area. "Nothing on fire." The empty WIND column. "Zones of confidence A/B/C" for seed sources. "The chart ends here. The web doesn't." These are jokes that only land if you've done the job, which is exactly the right audience test.
- The HW/LW/slack-water framing of a commit graph. It's a better contributions graph than GitHub's.
- The ship's log. I'd read a longer one.
- The phone edition being a real re-draw and not a squash.

### Pretentious

- The chart margin ritual everywhere: "CHART NO. 21 · SHEET N", "IALA REGION B", "marks numbered from seaward", "corrected through Notice 9", "Obstn rep. 2026 (PA)". Any one of them is charming; all of them together start to feel like the costume is insisting on itself. Someone who doesn't know what an Admiralty chart looks like will just see noise.
- "Notices to mariners" as a vehicle for engineering principles is fine; "The surface is part of the system… its earlier editions read as a template" is the page reviewing itself, and I winced a little.
- The symbols key on Sheet 3 is sixty entries long. Nobody reads a sixty-entry key. It's there to prove the metaphor is complete, not to help me.

### Missing

- A one-line "who I am" in plain words: student? working? where? looking for what? The page tells me what the person builds and almost nothing about their situation. Even "sophomore, CS" or "available from June" would change how I read all of it.
- Any external signal: stars, users, downloads, a blog post, a talk, someone else's repo they contributed to. Everything on the page is self-reported and self-generated. The 1,665 / 1,966 / 1,828 reconciliation is honest, but it's honest about a number nobody else has verified.
- A screenshot or GIF of Scrapy Harbor's Grafana dashboard, or rustmapper's real output. The ship's log is "computed from settings". One real artefact — even an ugly one — would anchor the whole chart.
- A date on the page as a whole that isn't in-fiction. "Redrawn nightly" and "7 Oct 2026" are there but in chart margin type; I want a plain "last updated".

### Would I want to work with this person?

Yes, with one eyebrow up. The rustmapper and Scrapy Harbor bullets show someone who thinks about failure first, states caveats without being asked (the Apple-silicon-only wheel, "provisional", "unsigned"), and has taste. The eyebrow: this page took real effort — layout for two viewports, an SVG system, nightly redraws — and I'd want to know whether the crawler got the same hours the chart did. The Instruments sheet quietly says everything is a year old, which makes both the ambition and the eyebrow reasonable.

### The one thing I would change

Move the Instruments sheet (first-commit dates) and the plain "Ben Russell builds…" paragraph *above* the big title chart, or fold the dates into the chart margin where the IALA flavour currently sits. The page's biggest risk is that a stranger forms their opinion in the first five seconds from the costume, and the costume says "lots of islands, lots of numbers" while the truth is "thirteen months, two serious repos, a real package." Leading with the honest frame makes the chart read as wit; leading with the chart makes the honest frame read as a confession.

## After reading README.md once — what the page intended that I did not get

- **The page is animated and I never saw it.** The hero's alt text says "A boat sails in and anchors"; the colophon says the boat sails in once in the first half-minute and then "only the lights keep time"; the Approaches alt says "the soundings fill in behind the vessel"; the log's alt says "only the cursor keeps time". I reviewed stills, so the one piece of theatre the author built — the survey being *performed* in front of you, with the depth numbers appearing in the vessel's wake — was invisible. That reframes my "soundings in URLs are texture pretending to be data" complaint: they're meant to appear as the boat passes, which is a much better idea than the static result suggests. It also means the static/reduced-motion edition, which is what a screenshot, a crawler, a PDF export or a reduced-motion user gets, needs to carry the meaning on its own, and right now it doesn't quite.
- **There is a night edition.** `<picture>` sources for `prefers-color-scheme: dark` on every sheet ("after dark, a night sheet on which the lights are the brightest things"). I only saw day. Can't judge it, but the existence of a separately-designed night sheet rather than an inverted one is the kind of care I'd have given credit for.
- **The compass rose is data, not flavour.** The colophon explains the inner ring is a 24-hour clock of commits with the arrow at the busiest hour, and "VAR 14h (2026)" is that hour. I wrote it off as decoration in five seconds and again in thirty. The information is good (someone who commits at 14:00 is not the same person as someone who commits at 02:00) but the chart does nothing to tell a stranger the rose is readable. One tiny caption — "busiest hour 14:00" — would have done it.
- **The numeral typography is a system.** "Upright numerals are measured; italic numerals are not; an underlined figure is above datum." I never noticed italics vs upright. Nobody will. It's there for the author's own integrity, which I respect, but the key on Sheet 3 that says "265 Upright · measured / 12 Sloping · not measured" was explaining a distinction I couldn't perceive at render size.
- **The chart number charts the chart.** "The chart number includes this repository; the chart is one of the things it charts." Cute recursion, 21 repos including the profile repo itself. I assumed 21 was arbitrary.
- **Everything is generated nightly from stats.json, and it is a template someone else can fork.** The HTML comment at the top, the `chart.toml` references, the "to draw your own, fork the repository" line. So the thing I eyebrow-raised at — "did the crawler get the hours the chart did?" — is partly answered: the chart is itself a build system with a checker (`check.py`) that refuses to publish a sheet that breaks a rule. That's not vanity, that's a small product. It would have changed my read of the Colophon if it hadn't been collapsed.
- **The "position" and "contact" slots are empty on purpose.** `<!-- position:start -->…<!-- position:end -->` and the LinkedIn/resume slots are wired in but blank. So the biggest thing I said was missing — a plain line about who this person is and what they're looking for — is a one-line edit in `chart.toml`, and the author already built the slot for it. Fill it.
- **The alt text is excellent and I never saw it.** "Chart of Ben Russell's 21 repositories, titled: I survey a web that is wrong about itself. Right margin unsurveyed. A boat sails in and anchors." and "Tide table of commits: high water 358… Every week sounded." A screen-reader user gets a better five-second summary than I did. That's rare and worth saying.
- **Below the waterline is richer than its summary.** The collapsed list has eleven described repos (a MapKit globe, Spotify-to-Apple-Music via ISRC, a voxel engine in C, an HVAC parts search over supplier APIs with zero-shot matching, a Qwen-on-MLX DB advisor). "Swift widgets, a C game engine, games, tooling" undersells it; the HVAC and ISRC ones are the kind of practical, slightly weird projects that make a person more hireable, not less.

What I'd keep from my original verdict: the costume still front-runs the content in the first five seconds for someone who doesn't know what an Admiralty chart is, the sixty-entry key is still for the author rather than the reader, and the one thing I'd change still stands — but I'd now say it differently. The page doesn't need the honest frame moved up; it needs the two empty slots filled and the three hidden systems (compass = hour clock, numerals = measured vs not, motion = the survey happening) given one visible word each, so that the stranger reading the still gets what the colophon reader gets.
