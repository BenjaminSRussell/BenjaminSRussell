## 1. As a profile

**5 seconds.** A cream nautical chart, "Ben Russell" very large, one italic line: "I survey a web that is wrong about itself." Then the eye goes to the biggest mass, which is **GAME ENGINE I.**, then Data Science Bank, then the compass. The flagship, rustmapper, is a 10 px glyph labelled PA at the right margin. Verdict a stranger forms: "a designer-ish developer who does something with the web." Not "crawl infrastructure". The sheet is honest to its unit (commits) and the unit says the wrong thing about the thesis: a game engine written in a month outweighs the crawler by 6:1. Nothing on screen one says what you do, what you're looking for, or where to click. The GitHub sidebar (avatar, bio, followers) is competing for the same 5 s and currently wins the "what is this person" question.

**30 seconds.** The link row and the three bold lines under the hero do the job cleanly: crawl and data infra, Python/Rust, stack, email. Then Soundings tells them, in large type, "typical week 0", "LW 0", "slack water Jul", and that the biggest repos are games. A hiring manager reads: early-career, bursty, side projects. That reading is *correct* and it is the page's own choice to foreground it on screen two. No world-class profile I know leads with its contribution cadence; GitHub already shows the green graph lower on the same page.

**2 minutes.** The rustmapper bullets (governor on redb commit latency, CRC32-framed WAL with rkyv replay, rendezvous-hashed frontier, three seed sources) and the Scrapy Harbor bullets are the best material on the page: specific, checkable, un-templated. Then the reader is handed a terminal log that is synthetic ("computed from settings · unsigned" — a convention they don't know), five aphorisms citing 2025 side projects, a legend of 36 symbols that decodes to "Redis = queues", a tool list drawn as a picture, a colophon about typefaces, and a footer whose boat bobs forever. They leave knowing: one Rust crawler at 0.1.3 alpha with one wheel, one Scrapy platform, and a great deal about the chart.

**What the best engineer profiles do that this doesn't.** They are short (one to two screens). They say in line one what the person does and what they want next (the `position.text` slot here is `""`). They make you click *into* a repo, because the repo is the evidence; everything on the profile is an index. They show one real artifact — a real dashboard screenshot, a real run, a real output file — with domain numbers (pages/s, hosts, p95 fetch, bytes stored), never commit counts. They let pinned repos carry weight. **What this does that they don't:** a genuinely one-of-a-kind visual identity; a provenance discipline (upright/italic, PA/ED/SD) most dashboards lack; night, phone and reduced-motion editions; alt text that is a sentence. The hero is the asset. Everything after it dilutes it.

**Amount of page.** Wrong by 2–3×. Six sheets, two `<details>`, nine "notices", a colophon: ~4,900 px desktop, ~12,000 px phone. Profiles are read in one scroll; the sheets after the hero are each a new thing to decode, with a new legend, in a conceit the reader already got. Target: ≤ 2,200 px desktop, one generated image.

## 2. Rendering reality

- **github.com desktop.** Works. `<picture>` + `<source media>` + `width="100%"` survive the sanitizer; images go through camo (SVG passes, 5 MB cap is far away), raw's 300 s cache means a nightly publish is visible within minutes. Two real degradations: (a) no `height` on the `<img>`, so six 50–200 KB SVGs arriving in sequence make the page jump as each one lays out; (b) the 767 px breakpoint is on the *viewport*, so a desktop window at 768–1010 px gets the desk sheet in a ~700 px column, where the 0.68 scale factor the type floors were tuned for becomes ~0.55 and semantic type lands at ~10 px.
- **Dark mode.** `prefers-color-scheme` follows the OS/browser, not the GitHub theme picker; a user on "dark" with a light OS (common) gets the day sheet on a dark page, and vice versa. Nothing you can do about it, but the design should assume it: the cream sheet must look intentional on #0d1117, and the night sheet must look intentional on light. Also GitHub has three darks (#0d1117, dimmed #22272e, high-contrast #010409); the night paper reads as a slab on dimmed. Framed sheets survive; the three unframed ones don't.
- **GitHub mobile app.** WebView, `<picture>` honoured, prefers-color-scheme honoured. The phone editions are correctly redrawn, not shrunk — good work — but the page is 15 screens, the Approaches phone sheet alone is ~4 screens, and its legend is the one thing a phone reader will not read.
- **Reduced motion.** Handled correctly by `<source>`. But still editions exist for Soundings and Instruments, which have zero animations: four redundant files, and a copy of every sheet a reviewer has to check.
- **SMIL in `<img>`.** Fine in Chrome, Firefox, Safari, including iOS. The real problem isn't support, it's *timing*: the timeline is "seconds from each image's load", 96 s long; the hero boat sails 4–28 s, Approaches' opening ends ~42 s. A reader scrolls past the hero in 5 s and reaches Approaches at maybe 20 s. Nobody sees a sail complete, and most see Approaches mid-opening or finished. The "every frame is a finished sheet" rule makes this harmless, which is also an admission that the motion carries nothing. The footer's 15 animations loop indefinitely; it is the only sheet that costs a repaint when the reader is sitting at the bottom of the page.
- **Cache coherence.** "Nothing is half-published" is true in git and false at the CDN: six URLs expire independently, so for up to five minutes after a publish the page can show yesterday's hero and today's soundings with different dates. Nobody will notice; I note it because the workflow comment claims otherwise.
- **Outlined text.** The identity, thesis, every number and label are paths. Not selectable, not findable with Ctrl-F, invisible to screen readers (alt carries one sentence) and to anyone who pastes a profile into a hiring note. That is the price of "looks the same on every machine" and it is the right trade for a hero; it is the wrong trade for a tool list, a log and a legend.
- **700 KB of SVG.** Wire cost is fine (~143 KB gz, less than one hero PNG at 2×). The cost is raster: 1,468 `<use>` of glyph outlines re-rastered on every SMIL tick at DPR 2–3. The perf harness exists because this is a real risk; the honest conclusion is that only the hero earns that budget.

The `<picture>` scheme is sound. It's just applied to five sheets that shouldn't exist.

## 3. Methodology

**Nightly regeneration from live repo data: right machine, wrong target.** "Generated from the real repos" is the one claim that makes the hero more than a drawing, and the survey code (clone, identity-filter, author-local hours, PyPI, provenance) is good data engineering — keep it. But nothing a reader can perceive changes nightly; nobody visits a profile twice a day. Nightly buys never-stale numbers at the cost of a cron, a force-push, a pencil-note fallback branch of the workflow, and a job that can go red while you're asleep. On-push plus weekly gives the same page. And the machine's output is pointed at the wrong quantities: commit counts, week-by-week cadence, first-commit dates of tools. None of these are what a crawl-infrastructure engineer is judged on. The one dataset that would support the thesis — a real crawl: hosts, URLs, throughput, p95, bytes, WAL replays — is the one the pipeline *simulates* (`logsim.py`). A page whose honesty conventions are this careful should not have, as its only demo, a fabricated terminal session, however scrupulously italicised.

**The rubric loop is polishing a local optimum.** J1–J12 (thesis once, cover the name, silhouette, region/unit/datum, legend, changes with data, honesty, still finished, no borrowed jokes, recruiter in 60 s, night designed, second-look layers) are ten criteria about fidelity to the conceit and two about the reader. Every criterion presupposes six sheets; no persona can score "cut four of them", so three rounds have produced better-drawn sheets and the same page. The rubric does not measure: what a stranger says Ben does after 30 s, whether they clicked a repo, what fraction of the page is about the work versus about the chart, or page length. The owner's verdict after round three ("I still don't like the design and methodology") is the loop's objective function disagreeing with the owner's. The surest tell: the profile repo is the fourth-largest island (173 commits, twice rustmapper), the tooling is ~17k lines, and docs/crit is larger than any product repo's README. The method has become the subject.

**Keep:** `build_stats.py` and `scripts/data/` (the survey, identity filter, provenance); `sheets/hero.py` and its contour/area solver; `typeset.py`/`tokens.py` (the outlined-font and floors machinery, scoped to the hero); the `<picture>` writer in `render_readme.py`; the fast tier of `check.py` (xml, size, strings, alt, contrast, bounds, expiry); the honesty conventions. **Throw away or shelve:** soundings, approaches, log, instruments, footer sheets and their 30 files; `logsim.py` (replace with a recorded run or nothing); the 96 s page timeline and `timeline.py`'s cross-sheet cues (hero keeps its 4–28 s sail); still editions for sheets without motion; the render and perf tiers in CI (keep `perf_check.js` as a dev tool); the persona panel and 12-point rubric as the acceptance test. Replace the latter with three humans given 30 s and one question ("what does he build, and would you click?"), plus two numbers in the build report: page height and seconds of motion.

## 4. The page I would ship

1. **Hero sheet** (generated, nightly or on-push, 8 files: day/night × desk/phone × moving/still). Keep the archipelago, the 24-h rose, the unsurveyed margin, the one italic thesis line, the boat sailing in once and anchoring; nothing moves after 28 s. Drop "sitemap.xml lies again — see Sheet 3". Re-weight editorially, not dishonestly: chart.toml already gives rustmapper and Scrapy their slots; let the chart's *lit* and *named* features be the crawl work and push the games to unnamed marks or a "small-scale inset" with the title saying so. Area ∝ commits can stay if the title says SOUNDINGS IN COMMITS; the eye should still land on the harbour.
2. **One line, written:** "Crawl and data infrastructure · Python and Rust · *currently …* / *open to …*" + rustmapper · Scrapy Harbor · PyPI · email · résumé/LinkedIn. Fill `position.text`; it's the line a hiring manager came for.
3. **rustmapper** (written): the four existing bullets, the `pip install` block, and **one real artifact**: a recorded run against a host you're allowed to crawl, as a plain `<pre>` or a PNG of the real terminal, with real URLs/s, hosts, p95 and WAL size. Upright numbers because they are measured. This replaces Ship's log and the Approaches sheet.
4. **Scrapy Harbor** (written): the four bullets, plus either nothing or a plain architecture diagram (stage → Delta Lake / Redis / PostgreSQL / Grafana) with real labels — not a harbour with a legend.
5. **Other work** (written): the four "other waters" lines, then `<details>` with the fifteen. Instruments becomes one line of text or is cut; the stack line under the hero already carries it.
6. **One-line colophon in text, next to the hero, not collapsed:** "Chart redrawn from my repositories by `scripts/build_assets.py` · how it's drawn." The pipeline is a credential only where it is seen.

Generated: the hero. Written: everything else. Moves: the boat, once. Cut: Soundings, Approaches, Ship's log (unless real), Instruments, Footer, Notices 4–9 (fold 1–3 into the project bullets as the sentences they already are), the Colophon details. Target ≤ 2,200 px desktop, ≤ 5,000 px phone, one legend-free screen after the hero.
