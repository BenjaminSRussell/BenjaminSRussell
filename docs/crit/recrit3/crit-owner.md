# Owner's advocate review — v91

Renders read: readme-light/dark (4 slices each), readme-390 (7 slices), the six 870 px sheets day and night, plus 2x crops of the hero's neatline bars, the Approaches neatline, and the key/value table. Data cross-checked against `assets/stats.json`, `assets/log.json`, `chart.toml`.

## 1. Readings

**Five seconds.** A cream nautical chart, "Ben Russell" in a very large serif, an italic line "I survey a web that is wrong about itself", blue islands with numbers, a compass. The stranger thinks: a chart-themed developer, something about surveying the web, probably crawlers. The conceit lands at once and it is unlike any other profile on GitHub; that novelty is what makes the first screen striking. Composition is weaker than novelty: the eye goes from the name to the largest island, which is GAME ENGINE I. (512), then to Data Science Bank (308); SCRAPY HARBOR is caps but sits in a dashed inset lower left, and the survey vessel (rustmapper, the flagship on PyPI) is a 10 px glyph. If they scroll one inch, the nav row and the table hand them "crawl and data infrastructure" in plain text, so the field is recovered within the five seconds. But the hero on its own says "surveyor" more clearly than it says "crawl infrastructure".

**One minute.** They can name both flagships, the two lead languages (Python, Rust), the stack, and an email; the table does that job cleanly. The rustmapper bullets are the best material on the page: governor on redb commit latency, CRC32-framed WAL with rkyv replay, rendezvous hashing by registrable domain, three seed sources. Concrete, checkable, and unlike a template. Then the page starts to cost them: the same six rustmapper facts and six Scrapy Harbor facts sit twice within one screen (Sheet 3's NOTES panels and the bullets under them); the thesis has been said five times by the time they reach Approaches (hero line, hero subtitle, table row, paragraph, Sheet 3 aside); Soundings tells them, honestly, that most weeks are zero and the busiest week and the deepest island are games; Instruments tells them the only instrument "underway this quarter" is TypeScript. Notices 1–3 read as earned principles; 4 is a title with no body citing a 37-commit repo, 5 cites the README itself, and 6–9 are four PyPI uploads in 74 minutes. Other waters sells 4- and 8-commit repos with paragraph-length blurbs, and a reader who has just seen Sheet 2 discounts the list. They leave trusting the page's honesty and the engineering, and a little tired of being told.

## 3. Findings, ranked

1. **The datum crowns the wrong features.** *Where:* hero (GAME ENGINE I. 512₁ is the largest island, Data Science Bank 308₄ second, Scrapy Harbor 265₅ and rustmapper 87₆ smaller); Sheet 2 top row (512, 308, 265); tide HW 358 "Data Science Bank"; Instruments bold = TypeScript only. *Why:* the owner wants the page to say what he does; the data layer says his biggest work is an 8-day game engine (two days of 201 and 246 commits) and a browser game with a 358-commit day. A hiring manager who reads the chart as intended reads that. *Change:* keep it honest by changing the unit, not the data. `stats.json` already has `commit_days`: Scrapy 26, Rust-sitemap 22, Data_science_dev 11, BenjaminSRussell 9, game_engine 8. Soundings in commit-days put the flagships deepest, and every number stays measured. Unit label, legend subscripts and the Sheet 2 table column follow. *Effort:* M.

2. **Sheet 3 and the prose under it say the same twelve things.** *Where:* the rustmapper and Scrapy Harbor NOTES panels on Approaches, then the bullets directly below (hashed by registrable domain, 256–1024 permits, 250 ms, WAL crc32/rkyv, seeds A·B·C, cp313 wheel; Arrow schemas, schema_mode=merge, MinHash, OPTIMIZE/VACUUM, breakers, BART). *Why:* it is the single largest "told twice" on the page and it sits inside one screen. *Change:* drop the two NOTES panels from Sheet 3 (the chart, legend and zones stay; the sheet gets its height back) and keep the bullets, which are the searchable plain text. *Effort:* M.

3. **The thesis paragraph is the caption the rules forbid.** *Where:* "Most of what I build is survey work. The sitemap and the site disagree… the crawlers go and take soundings… a dashboard says where the boat is…" *Why:* it explains the conceit in prose after the hero has already shown it, and it is the fourth statement of the thesis before Soundings. *Change:* cut the paragraph; if the last sentence is wanted, move it to the Colophon. *Effort:* S.

4. **The ship's log prints invented performance figures.** *Where:* Sheet 4, 1405 line: "p95 812 ms · failed 0.4% · wal fsync 3 ms", "1h 43m". `assets/log.json` has `measured: false`, `source: computed`, and the figures typed into a `profile` block. *Why:* "computed from settings · unsigned" at the bottom right is the only flag, and a p95 cannot be computed from settings; this breaks the owner's own no-invented-figures rule on the one sheet that looks most like evidence. *Change:* either run a real crawl of a fixture site in the workflow and sign the log (`measured: true`, "closed by B.S.R."), or cut the 1405 health line to the fields that really are settings (permits, shards, delay, rate). *Effort:* L for the real run, S for the cut.

5. **The neatline strikes through the sheet-number bar.** *Where:* Sheet 1 bottom ("CHART NO. 21 · SHEET 1 · SMALL CORRECTIONS 2026 — 169–173" and "GITHUB.COM/…") and Sheet 3 bottom ("CHART NO. 21 · SHEET 3 · APPROACHES…"); the dashed border runs through the text at 870 px. *Why:* it reads as struck-through, and a chart prints its number clear of the neatline. *Change:* move the bar below the border or the border above the bar. *Effort:* S.

6. **Notices 6–9 and Notice 5 pad the count.** *Where:* "Notices 6–9, editions: rustmapper 0.1.0 · 0.1.1 · 0.1.2 · 0.1.3 · 8 Nov 2025"; hero "CORRECTED THROUGH NOTICE 9"; footer "9 notices · corrected through Notice 9"; Notice 5 cites "This page, Nov 2025". *Why:* a reader who clicks finds four uploads between 18:38 and 19:52 on one day and re-reads "9 notices" as inflation; a principle whose only evidence is the README itself reads as posturing next to Notices 1–3, which are earned. *Change:* count the four editions as one notice (or none) so hero and footer say 5 or 6; cut Notice 5 or move its citation to the Colophon. *Effort:* S.

7. **Other waters oversells against Sheet 2.** *Where:* mlx_Qwen_data_entry (4 commits, 2 commit-days), rust_llm_logger (8), Ai_code_detector (22, "probabilistic forensics across seven languages"), Elusive_trades_data (17, off-thesis HVAC). *Why:* the reader has just seen the soundings; blurbs longer than the repos' histories make the whole list read as a template "other projects" block. *Change:* keep ideal-url-organizer (cited by Notice 4) and go_go_go (sibling of the flagship), at most rust_llm_logger; move the rest below the waterline with their one-line entries. *Effort:* S.

8. **Instruments is the sheet a world-class edit would cut.** *Where:* Sheet 5: rotated LEAD / LOG / LOOKOUT take 40% of the width; the content is the table's Stack row plus first-commit dates; the only bold "underway this quarter" item is TypeScript. *Why:* it is the one sheet that looks generated, it repeats plain text already on the page, and its single emphasis points away from the thesis. *Change:* cut the sheet and let the table carry the stack (the dates can live as a subscript in Sheet 2's repository table if wanted); at minimum drop the bold-this-quarter rule. *Effort:* M to cut, S to drop the bold.

9. **Sheet 2's figures are printed twice, adjacent.** *Where:* the sheet footer "Source · 21 public repositories cloned, author's commits only · 1,665 commits · 1,966 all hands · 1,828 by GitHub's calendar" and the `<sub>` line under it "1,665 commits of mine · 1,966 all hands · 21 repositories surveyed · since Oct 2024 · soundings taken 7 Oct 2026"; the date also sits in the sheet header. *Why:* J9. *Change:* keep the `<sub>` (plain text) and cut the figures from the sheet footer, leaving the source clause. *Effort:* S.

10. **Table header and nav finish.** *Where:* the key/value table renders a blank header row (light, dark and phone); "How it's built" in the primary nav links DESIGN.md, which the Colophon already links, and on the phone the slot orphans to a second line with a leading dot. *Why:* the blank row is the first unfinished thing a reader meets after the hero; the nav slot is where a recruiter expects LinkedIn or a résumé, and it currently points at the page's own design doc. *Change:* make "Ben Russell builds" the header row (or an HTML `<dl>`); drop "How it's built" from the nav. *Effort:* S.

11. **Repository count does not reconcile.** *Where:* 2 flagships + 6 Other waters + 15 Below the waterline = 23; the chart surveys 21 including the profile repo (20 projects). ggml-viz, Boxalarm-monorepo and excel-and-vba are hand-written in README.md and absent from `stats.json`. *Why:* a careful reader counts; a chart that says "21 repositories" next to a list of 23 undercuts the survey claim. *Change:* if those three are forks or empty, drop them and change "15 more" to the real number; if they are real, let the survey pick them up. *Effort:* S.

12. **Sheet 3's URL field is decoration.** *Where:* the sloped soundings 18, 20, 23 … 44 (thousands) across rustmapper's survey ground; legend "Sloping · not measured". *Why:* the only measured URL figure on the page is the log's 12,440 on one host; a field of not-measured numbers on the sheet that is supposed to show the survey edge invites the question "measured where?" *Change:* derive the field from the same real run as finding 4, or thin it to the few soundings that shape the channel and leave the ground blank as "unsurveyed". *Effort:* M.

**Present that a world-class profile would not have:** the thesis paragraph (3), the NOTES panels duplicating the prose (2), Notices 5–9 (6), Instruments (8). **Missing:** nothing a recruiter needs, since field, stack, languages and email are in plain text; role, location and LinkedIn are empty slots by choice. The engineering bullets under Approaches are the strongest thing on the page; the fixes above mostly clear space around them.
